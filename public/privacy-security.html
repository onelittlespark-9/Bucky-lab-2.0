Privacy, security & acceptable use

Educational simulator. Bucky Lab 2.0 must not be used to upload, enter or process identifiable patient information, special-category health information, credentials or other confidential personal information. The published teaching datasets are intended to be anonymised, source-derived or synthetic educational material.

Privacy and UK data protection

The service is designed around data minimisation and does not require users to provide patient data to use the simulator. Where infrastructure necessarily processes limited technical information for security, delivery or logging, it should be limited to what is necessary, protected by appropriate technical and organisational measures, retained only as necessary, and handled consistently with applicable UK data-protection law. A deployment operator remains responsible for determining its controller/processor obligations, lawful basis, retention, processor contracts and data-subject procedures.

Authorised use

Access is provided only for authorised educational use. Users must not attempt to obtain unauthorised access to systems or non-public resources; bypass authentication or security controls; probe, scan or exploit vulnerabilities without prior written authorisation; interfere with availability or integrity; introduce malicious code; obtain credentials; access or alter data without permission; or use the service to facilitate attacks against other systems.

Intellectual property and reuse

Access to the hosted application does not itself grant permission to copy, redistribute, republish, reverse engineer, create derivative versions of, or commercially exploit Bucky Lab software or Bucky Lab-created content. Any rights in third-party datasets remain governed by their respective licences and cannot be restricted by this notice beyond those licence terms. Any separate rights granted by applicable law also remain unaffected. Where permission is required for Bucky Lab material, obtain it from the rights holder before reuse.

Security response

Suspected abuse may result in access being blocked, rate-limited, investigated and logged where lawful and proportionate. Evidence of suspected unauthorised access or interference may be preserved and referred to the relevant service provider, organisation or lawful authority where appropriate. This notice does not automatically make every breach a criminal offence; legal consequences depend on the facts and applicable law.

No security guarantee

No internet service can guarantee that unauthorised access is impossible. Security controls must be maintained, tested and updated in proportion to risk. Vulnerabilities should be reported privately to the operator rather than exploited or publicly disclosed before remediation.
